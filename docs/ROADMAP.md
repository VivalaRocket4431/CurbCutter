# Roadmap

## Version 1 (this release): the private toolkit

Everything runs on the person's phone: documenting, sourced letters, the escalation path, reminders, local contacts for DFW, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin, and the rights library. There are no accounts and no server.

## Version 1.1: reach

- **Spanish**, translated and reviewed by fluent speakers.
- **More cities:** Arlington, Plano, Irving, Garland, Pasadena, Sugar Land, Round Rock, New Braunfels, and others, each with checked 311, ADA office, and council contacts.
- **More barrier types:** service animals turned away, inaccessible communication, and websites.
- **Dallas and Fort Worth volunteer parking enforcement**, once the current programs are confirmed with each city.

## Version 2: collective pressure

- **A shared map of reported barriers**, with co-signing so one business hears from many customers. This needs a database, sign-in, photo moderation, and rules against naming private people, so it should be designed with disability advocates before it's built.
- **Anonymous, opt-in impact numbers**, such as letters sent and barriers fixed, to show the app's results without tracking anyone.

## Partners to approach

- **C+C** (the "Advocacy in Action" presenters) and **BIVC**: early testers, feedback, and outreach to members.
- **Centers for Independent Living** (REACH, the Coalition for Barrier Free Living, SAILS, ARCIL): referrals, and help for people who want support.
- **The Southwest ADA Center**: review of the business-facing language, and a place to send businesses with questions.
- **City volunteer parking programs** (ParkHouston, Austin, SAPD): link reports to volunteers who can act.

## How to measure impact (for the portfolio)

- Barriers fixed, from stories people choose to share at hello@curbcutter.org.
- Time from first request to fix, counted only with consent.
- Testing sessions with people who have brain injuries: can they finish a report alone, and how long does it take?
